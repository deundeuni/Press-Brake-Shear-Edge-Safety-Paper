 * Structural Limitations, Empirical Failure Modes, and Potential Operational Risk Analysis of Pinch Hazards in Press Brakes, Shears, and Derivative Sheet Metal Machinery
 * Official pinch point machine classification by the Korea Occupational Safety and Health Agency and OSHA 1910.212/217 — Presses, shears (cutters), forming machines, press brakes (bending machines), roll benders, pipe benders, notchers, ironworkers (combined machines), and roll formers are designated as representative machinery generating high-load pinch points, nip points, and dangerous Points of Operation in industrial sites, strictly requiring physical and electrical safety devices compliant with ANSI B11.3, ANSI B11.12, and OSHA regulations.
 * Actual Failure Mode 1 — Accidents during manual work after temporarily disabling safety optical sensors due to alarm fatigue during micro-cutting/notching under 10mm and roller intake work — During residual cutting, small corner processing on shears/press brakes/notchers, or initial material in-feed on roll benders/roll formers, the existing simple optical sensor method often misjudges normal working fingers or feeding materials as hazards, causing frequent nuisance trips. This is the root cause of the practice where workers physically bypass or mask optical sensors and perform manual work defenselessly, leading to a repeated pattern of finger amputation and crushing accidents caused by unexpected strokes or roller bites.
 * Actual Failure Mode 2 — Free fall by dead weight and rotational inertia during sudden power outages, and automatic restart malfunctions after power restoration — A serious structural disaster pattern is reported where, during a momentary power outage while operating a press/press brake/ironworker, the upper die free-falls due to its own weight caused by hydraulic pressure loss, or the roll bender roller rotates due to inertia. Upon power restoration, the controller's auto-restart setting causes unexpected strokes, injuring workers who were cleaning or inspecting between the dies or rollers.
 * Actual Failure Mode 3 — Entanglement and pinch accidents due to continuous rolling/bending work fatigue and prolonged accumulation of shocks and vibrations — During large sheet metal roll bending or continuous pressing/shearing work, strong shocks and rotational vibrations from the machine are transmitted to the worker's body, inducing musculoskeletal fatigue and a loss of concentration. Accidents frequently occur when workers attempt to manually correct sheet metal spring-back without cutting off the power, getting entangled in the rollers or dies.
 * Mitigation of potential operational adverse effects and application of modern safety governance — This system operates not as a tool to monitor and track worker behavior, but as a "Quiet Auxiliary" that provides a foundational environment for safe operation by constantly assisting and checking the worker's condition and safety posture. Following the soma-moa philosophy, Heinrich's concept (1931) is modernly reinterpreted, applying Safety-II and Just Culture to flexibly supplement the continuity of the overwhelming majority of normal operations rather than merely suppressing accidents, thereby proactively mitigating the risk of safety device neutralization caused by backlash against workplace surveillance.
 * Strengthening institutional responsibility and practical limitations in the field — Although preventing pinch accidents is urgent under the Serious Accidents Punishment Act and OSHA General Duty regulations, most sites face the burden of system reconstruction and operational downtime risks associated with replacing equipment with heavy, high-reliability machinery or completely modifying the main PLC circuits.
 * Inevitability of Self-Computation and Local Physical Control of Edge and On-Device Terminals
 * Non-invasive Silent Auxiliary system and unused terminal linkage structure — In compliance with the IEC 60204-1 open standard for electrical equipment of industrial machines, the system aims for a lightweight structure where an independent, lightweight module flexibly controls the contact signal (Motor EN / Relay) only during emergencies, without directly modifying the main equipment's PLC or firmware. This is achieved by directly connecting to unused safety option terminals (e.g., X3/X4 spare slots) left by the manufacturer, sniffing the existing optical sensor OSSD photocouplers, or inserting a dry contact (non-powered contact) in series within the emergency stop/safety relay loop.
 * Ensuring independent safety assistance when existing safeguards are bypassed/muted — Even if the main optical sensors or interlock guards are manually bypassed or automatically muted to perform special processes on cutters/press brakes/roll benders/notchers, this non-invasive module operates independently of the main PLC's control state, uniquely continuing to execute cutoff control when a worker physically enters a hazard zone via triple sensor fusion and eFPGA interlocks.
 * Separation of roles between ultra-low latency hardware interlocks and local AI — Emergency cutoffs are directly controlled at the terminal local eFPGA circuit level with an execution speed of 0.1ms, while the lightweight local AI (NPU) supplements the accuracy of the hardware interlocks by continuously updating risk weights and contextual analysis of sensor signals in the background pipeline. (Oriented towards IEC 61508/62061 functional safety).
 * Normally non-powered state-maintaining EPM/mechanical latch against power outages and power-lockout upon power restoration (linked to soma-moa L0/L1) — Upon main grid power failure, energy storage elements and normally non-powered state-maintaining Electro-Permanent Magnets (EPM) / mechanical latches (such as spring-based fail-safe brakes or pneumatic brakes) immediately actuate, physically damping and securing the free fall of the die and the inertial rotation of the roller without power after the state transition. Upon power restoration, the soma-moa L2 FSM RECOVERY latch state is automatically maintained, applying a Power Lockout governance where the machine is never arbitrarily restarted without the worker's manual approval (physical key/authentication).
 * Mitigation of false recognition through complex sensors and local multimodal analysis — To compensate for the vulnerabilities of single optical sensor methods, a 60GHz FMCW radar, a 32x24 thermal imaging camera, and a copper foil capacitance sensor are simultaneously applied with lightweight local AI models for vision and anomalous sound detection to precisely distinguish the processed material (sheet metal, pipe, shape steel) from the worker's body.
 * Ensuring air-gap and offline independent operation — Because it is driven by an independent power source and local computing unit regardless of external network connection status, it fully maintains its safety assist function even in communication loss or network failure environments.
 * 5mm Offset Auxiliary Finger, Immediate Upward/Reverse Recovery, and Quiet Assist Control Mechanism
 * 5mm offset auxiliary finger structure — In the area within 5mm of the V-die, shear blade, and roller intake, an auxiliary finger is deployed to supplement the ISO 13857 and ISO 13854 standard clearances. Utilizing 1.5T spring elasticity, it simultaneously performs physical support and push-away functions, physically mitigating the path through which the worker's body parts directly enter the hazardous die and roller nip zones.
 * Quiet Assist responding to manual micro-machining under 10mm and bypassing of existing devices for cutters/notchers/pipes — Even when existing safeguards are disabled during small fragment cutting, corner processing, or residual processing under 10mm in cutters, press brakes, notchers, and ironworkers, this system links with the Special-Purpose Tooling Mode. The triple sensors (thermal imaging 36-degree deviation, FMCW radar scattering angle, SCL capacitance) and lightweight AI track the distance between the worker's finger and the processed material in millimeters. Only when the finger directly enters the Point of Operation does it trigger the 0.1ms eFPGA motor stop and immediate upward/reverse recovery, simultaneously satisfying process continuity and safety assistance.
 * Smooth decelerated descent/rotation linkage, ultra-low latency cutoff, and immediate upward/reverse recovery logic to safety height (origin) — Linked to the EN 12622 slow bending/forming speed (Safe Speed 10mm/s or less and Mute Point, applied analogously to other derivative machinery) and ISO 13855 approach speed calculation formulas, it cuts off the motor drive with 3-way AND + eFPGA 0.1ms ultra-low latency as soon as an anomaly or the worker's dangerous posture is detected in the smoothly descending or rotating section. Upon cutoff, it fundamentally mitigates secondary pinch accidents caused by pressurization and nip inertia by immediately ramping up the upper die to a set safety height or reverse-rotating the roll bender roller.
   * Physical buffer (Damping of kinetic energy based on velocity squared ratio): Compared to a maximum speed of 200\,\text{mm/s}, driving at a Safe Speed of 10\,\text{mm/s} below the Mute Point significantly dampens the impact energy to a level of (10/200)^2 = 1/400 according to the kinetic energy proportional relationship (E_k = \frac{1}{2}m v^2). This prevents motor/hydraulic jam phenomena during sudden stops, secures the driving force for immediate upward origin recovery and roller reverse rotation, and mitigates secondary pinching and die/roller damage.
   * Psychological buffer: Predictable descent and rotation speeds suppress the worker's Startle Reflex, preventing sudden movements and assisting in maintaining a stable posture.
   * Ergonomic buffer (Occupational disease prevention): Flexibly dampening the risks of musculoskeletal disorders and chronic occupational diseases caused by strong mechanical shocks and rotational vibrations absorbed entirely by the worker's body during prolonged repetitive work assists the worker's long-term health rights and work sustainability.
 * Exception handling type safety assist for 5mm close-proximity bending/forming/rolling — It avoids unconditional blanket shutdowns when entering within 5mm of the V-die and roller nip points. The moment the upper die descends or the roller rotates, the triple sensors (60GHz scattering angle 30-40 degrees, 32x24 thermal imaging 36 degrees, SCL capacitance) determine in real-time whether the position and posture of the worker's hand/fingers are in a safe supporting posture (e.g., side support, holding the feed guide). If determined to be a safe posture, the operation is continuously maintained to preserve productivity; if the posture is disturbed or rapidly enters the danger zone during processing, an ultra-low latency stop and upward/reverse origin recovery are executed.
 * Alarm fatigue mitigation and risk differential calculation logic linked to die/roller shapes — In accordance with ANSI B11.3 and ANSI B11.12 risk assessment guidelines, this logic calculates risks differentially by recognizing upper/lower die profiles (gooseneck, offset, etc.), roller curvature/gaps, wiper die gaps, and stroke ranges to prevent nuisance trips where simple optical sensors misjudge even normal working postures during special shape die or roller processing. When the body is fixed in a safe clearance position that does not intersect the physical pinch line, false cutoffs are suppressed to induce the safety device to remain in a constantly active (ON) state, paradoxically preventing the worker from entirely neutralizing the safety device.
 * Dualized operational mode governance (Normal Safety Mode vs. Special-Purpose Tooling Mode) — Regardless of the production quantity, this system separates the control layers into two operational modes depending on the die/roller profile and micro-machining conditions used.
   * Normal Safety Mode — Activated during standard flat iron cutting, simple right-angle bending, and general punching work, executing strict physical protection via triple sensor fusion and 3-way AND eFPGA interlocks.
   * Special-Purpose Tooling Mode — Activated during all works applying non-standard/special shape dies (gooseneck, offset blades, diagonal blades, sash dies), large sheet metal roll bending, pipe bending, manual micro-cutting/notching under 10mm, and 5mm close-proximity bending, regardless of production volume. It can only be entered via UWB smart tag/security watch authentication or dedicated physical key operation. The 5mm offset auxiliary finger, T-Reg 15% output restriction, die/roller shape-linked risk weighting, independent auxiliary operation when bypassing existing safeguards, and immediate upward/reverse origin recovery upon posture deviation are complexly interlinked to simultaneously secure safety and field workability.
 * soma-moa Charter 0 and modern reinterpretation of safety framework (Safety-II & Just Culture) linkage — Inheriting the axiom that "System governance is the main, but it is auxiliary to the primary work of humans," it continuously monitors the worker's condition as an auxiliary and builds a foundation for a reassuring operational environment.
   * Safety-II Resilience (Hollnagel Resilience) — Achieves production efficiency and safety simultaneously by intensively assisting the continuity of the overwhelming majority of normal operations performed stably in the field, without being bogged down solely by accident prevention.
   * Just Culture and PII destruction within 10 seconds — Adopting Heinrich's 300:29:1 ratio as a preventive motive, logs of daily movements and minor familiarity lapses are permanently destroyed from the volatile buffer (RAM 3.2KB) within 10 seconds to prevent/mitigate indiscriminate surveillance misunderstandings. Only critical risk delta logs are locally preserved in an anonymized CBOR format to establish an autonomous safety culture.
   * Quiet haptic notification — Minimizes worker fatigue by utilizing a security watch haptic notification 1 time (caution) / 2 times (stop) recognized only by the worker themselves, replacing public noise warnings.
 * Fail-Safe default and risk assessment clues — The default setting of the system maintains strict Fail-Safe protection. Control governance is established such that exception weights linked to die and roller shapes and the Special-Purpose Tooling Mode are activated only when third-party verification, certification body risk assessments compliant with relevant safety standards (ISO 13849/16092, ANSI B11.19), or precise parameter approval procedures by an administrator precede them.
 * Overcoming Physical Constraints, Ultra-Low Latency Motor Stop, and T-Reg Governance
 * eFPGA-based ultra-low latency motor stop control — Upon detecting risk signs, the motor enable signal is controlled at the hardware circuit level without passing through software threads, immediately inducing a machine drive stop and rotation cutoff at an execution speed of 0.1ms.
 * T-Reg 15% Fail-PRELOCK mode — Upon entering a sensor detection boundary zone or activating the Special-Purpose Tooling Mode, the machine's operational output is forcibly degraded to a 15% level to mitigate the impact of sudden operations.
 * HORIZONTAL_HANDOVER and 80% proactive prelock — Performs horizontal handover when moving across multiple work zones (press lines, roller intakes, multi-point workstations), and triggers a proactive prelock when reaching 80% of the risk threshold to suppress the spread of risk.
 * Anonymous logging and PII destruction within 10 seconds — Upon event occurrence, generates anonymized logs in CBOR format excluding Personally Identifiable Information (PII), and permanently destroys raw data within 10 seconds immediately after recording to maintain security.
